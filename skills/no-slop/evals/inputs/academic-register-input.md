# Methods and Limitations

Participants (n = 214) were recruited through university mailing lists between March and June 2025. The sample skewed young (median age 22) and toward students in social science programmes, which may limit generalisability to other populations.

Our findings suggest that the intervention is associated with improved recall at the two-week follow-up. We cannot rule out that the effect is partly attributable to demand characteristics, as participants were aware of the study's aims. It should be noted that attrition was higher in the control condition (18%) than in the treatment condition (9%), which may have introduced bias.

These results may indicate a genuine effect, but replication with a pre-registered design and a blinded control would be required before any causal claim could be sustained. We do not claim that the intervention causes improved retention in naturalistic settings.
